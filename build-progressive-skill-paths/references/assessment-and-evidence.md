# Assessment and Evidence

Assess capability through performance, explanation, transfer, and autonomy.

## Assessment mix

Use all four modes for important skills:

1. **Knowledge:** explain mechanisms, vocabulary, boundaries, and trade-offs.
2. **Execution:** complete a representative task and verify the output.
3. **Transfer:** apply the concept when inputs, constraints, or context change.
4. **Autonomy:** plan, decide, seek feedback, recover, and document with appropriate independence.

Avoid relying on confidence, attendance, videos watched, copied output, or quiz scores alone.

## Gate rubric

Rate each criterion as `not shown`, `guided`, `independent`, or `adaptive`.

| Criterion | Evidence |
|---|---|
| Conceptual accuracy | Explanation, diagram, prediction, or design rationale |
| Functional result | Demonstration against acceptance criteria |
| Quality | Tests, review, performance, accessibility, or maintainability checks |
| Troubleshooting | Diagnosis, repair, and prevention of a failure |
| Transfer | Successful response to an unfamiliar variation |
| Judgment | Reasoned trade-offs, risk handling, and escalation choices |
| Communication | Clear documentation, handoff, or teaching |
| Autonomy | Appropriate independence for the target stage |

Pass a stage only when critical safety and correctness criteria are met and the target behavior is shown more than once, including one varied case.

## Feedback format

Give feedback in this order:

1. outcome versus acceptance criteria;
2. strongest demonstrated capability;
3. highest-risk gap;
4. smallest corrective exercise;
5. retest condition;
6. next unlocked task.

Make feedback specific to observable work. Replace “understand this better” with a task that would demonstrate the missing capability.

## Spaced evidence

Check important knowledge and skills after a delay:

- same day: immediate correction;
- next session: unaided recall or repeat;
- later milestone: integration in a larger project;
- different context: transfer;
- stage gate: independent demonstration.

Adjust intervals to the learner and schedule; the purpose is durable retrieval, not a rigid calendar.

## Portfolio quality

A credible portfolio artifact states:

- the problem and intended user;
- the learner's specific contribution;
- constraints and assumptions;
- important decisions and rejected alternatives;
- tests, measurements, and known limitations;
- feedback received and changes made;
- safe reproduction or demonstration instructions.

Do not expose secrets, private data, employer-confidential information, or unsafe operational details.
