# Resource Selection

Choose resources for the learner and use case, not by popularity alone.

## Source order

Prefer sources in this order, adapting for the domain:

1. official specifications, standards, and documentation;
2. recognized professional bodies and public institutions;
3. maintained reference implementations and official examples;
4. accredited or peer-reviewed curricula;
5. reputable structured courses with active maintenance;
6. realistic interactive labs or sandboxes;
7. high-quality books and long-form guides;
8. engineering case studies with measurable context;
9. trusted communities, mentors, and review channels;
10. tutorials, videos, and quick references used as supplements.

Do not assume this is a universal platform ranking. Search within the relevant domain and favor the primary publisher.

## Score candidates

Score every candidate out of 10:

| Criterion | Points | Question |
|---|---:|---|
| Authority | 0-3 | Is it produced or reviewed by the specification owner, professional body, recognized expert, or accountable organization? |
| Currency | 0-2 | Does it match the current stable version and show a maintenance date? |
| Hands-on value | 0-2 | Does it include exercises, feedback, realistic outputs, or a usable lab? |
| Level fit | 0-2 | Does its assumed knowledge and pace match the learner? |
| Accessibility | 0-1 | Is it affordable, available, navigable, and usable in the learner's context? |

Use the highest-scoring small set that covers different functions. Do not select five resources that all explain the same material.

## Build the stage stack

For each stage, select at most:

- **Anchor:** the authoritative reference to resolve ambiguity.
- **Guide:** the main structured explanation or course.
- **Lab:** the place to practice with feedback.
- **Case source:** one realistic project, dataset, incident, or problem collection.
- **Feedback:** a mentor, community, review process, or automated evaluator.

For each item state:

- exact title and publisher;
- direct link when available;
- what sections or exercises to use;
- stage and prerequisites;
- free/paid status and material limitations;
- version or last-verified date;
- why it earned a place in the path.

## Reject or warn

Reject a resource when it is unverifiable, misleading, copied without attribution, unsafe, or built around an obsolete version with no migration note.

Warn when it:

- teaches shortcuts without underlying concepts;
- has no meaningful practice or feedback;
- markets completion as expertise;
- requires unnecessary tools or subscriptions;
- hides important security, privacy, cost, or production constraints;
- encourages copying complete solutions before attempting the work.

## Current-resource check

When the request asks for current or 2026-aligned resources:

1. browse official publisher pages;
2. verify version, status, date, and canonical link;
3. distinguish current, previous, preview, and deprecated material;
4. cite sources near the relevant recommendation;
5. include `Verified on YYYY-MM-DD`;
6. state any uncertainty rather than inventing a ranking.
