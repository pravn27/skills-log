# Mastery Levels

Use these four stages as a practical translation layer across different competency frameworks. Adapt the examples to the domain; do not mechanically claim formal equivalence to SFIA, DigComp, EQF, or another framework.

## Level model

| Stage | Task context | Autonomy | Learner can | Suitable evidence |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Beginner | Simple, familiar, well-defined | Guided | Explain core terms, follow a safe procedure, reproduce a known result, recognize common errors | Annotated exercise, small lab, explanation, troubleshooting log |
| Intermediate (Medium) | Well-defined work with normal variation | Independent for routine work | Choose standard methods, build from a brief, test results, fix common failures, explain choices | End-to-end project, test evidence, runbook, code or work review |
| Advanced | Complex, unfamiliar, integrated | Independent; guides others | Compare approaches, handle ambiguity, optimize trade-offs, diagnose systemic failures, integrate multiple areas | Production-like capstone, architecture or decision record, incident analysis, peer review |
| Expert | Novel, high-impact, strategically ambiguous | Leads and is accountable | Create or adapt practices, set direction, manage risk, evaluate frontier ideas, mentor others, defend decisions with evidence | Sustained outcomes, original design, standards contribution, strategy, teaching and reviewed impact |

## Stage exit gates

### Beginner to Intermediate

Require the learner to:

- complete a representative task with limited prompts;
- explain the main mechanism in plain language;
- detect and repair one seeded error;
- reproduce the result later without copying every step;
- save an artifact and short reflection.

### Intermediate to Advanced

Require the learner to:

- deliver an end-to-end solution from acceptance criteria;
- choose among at least two reasonable approaches;
- test normal, boundary, and failure cases;
- explain security, reliability, cost, usability, or maintainability trade-offs;
- review another solution or respond constructively to review.

### Advanced to Expert

Require sustained evidence across more than one context:

- solve novel or ambiguous problems;
- create reusable guidance, automation, or architecture;
- lead decisions whose consequences are measured;
- teach, mentor, or improve team capability;
- update beliefs when evidence contradicts an initial position.

Do not award an Expert label from a single capstone, certificate, quiz score, or number of study hours.

## Progress dimensions

Track each dimension separately from 0 to 4:

| Score | Knowledge | Execution | Transfer | Autonomy |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | Cannot yet explain | Cannot yet perform | No transfer shown | Needs full direction |
| 1 | Recognizes and recalls | Performs by following steps | Repeats the demonstrated case | Works with close guidance |
| 2 | Explains relationships | Completes standard tasks | Handles a small variation | Works with checkpoints |
| 3 | Evaluates trade-offs | Handles complex tasks reliably | Adapts across contexts | Works independently and guides peers |
| 4 | Extends or creates knowledge | Improves systems and practice | Solves novel classes of problems | Leads responsibly under ambiguity |

Use the profile, not an average, to find gaps. A learner with strong theory but weak execution still needs hands-on practice.
